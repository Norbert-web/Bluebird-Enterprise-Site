What makes it distinctive

A desktop-inspired experience, designed for Android.

Bluebird OS is designed around the idea that an Android device can provide more than a traditional home-screen experience. The project combines a desktop-style shell with purpose-built applications and ongoing development.

Desktop ModeWindowed apps, taskbar and multitasking.
Built-in appsBrowser, Files, Terminal, Notes, Music and more.
Accessible hardware targetDesigned with budget and mid-range Android devices in mind.
Open developmentFree to use and developed in the open.

Remote Schools

Uganda's free curriculum-structured learning platform. What started as a school website project is growing into a full remote-learning platform, built around a reusable schools database: home, about, academics, admissions, student life, news & events, contact & support, and a parent/student portal for fees and internal resources.

Structure

Home → About → Academics

Mission, background, administration, and facilities grouped under one About section; academics covers curriculum, roadmap, performance, and downloads.

Structure

Admissions → Student Life

How to apply and fee info, alongside clubs, sports, the prefectorial body, and student-written articles and stories.

Structure

News, Contact & Portal

An events calendar, a contact and suggestion board, and a login-gated portal for fees and other sensitive records.

In development · free for Ugandan schools

Bluebird Schools Management System

A completely free, full-stack school management system built for the local context — student records, and administration, designed to run well even where connectivity is limited.

Feature

Local server management

Built to run on a school's own local server, so records stay accessible even with limited or no internet.

Feature

Student database

A structured, searchable record of students, results, and attendance, tailored to how Ugandan schools actually operate.

Feature

More on the way

Additional modules are in active development as the project matures.
